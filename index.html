The Future of Work:
Skills, AI & the New Organisation

We are living through a rewrite of how work gets done — not in decades, but in months. Over the next 45 minutes we'll unpack what's actually changing, which skills now matter most, and what shape the new organisation is taking.

⏱ 45 min 👥 Human + AI teams 🧭 Future of Jobs 2025 📈 Making it real

Subhankar Pattanayak

AI Strategist at SAP · Founder, TechSambad

Welcome · Guest Speaker

Why we're here

Every organisation is asking the same four questions

1.

Will AI take my role?

Around 1.1 billion jobs could be transformed by technology in the next decade. Rarely replaced whole — but every task mix will change.

2.

Which skills survive?

Analytical thinking is now the #1 most-sought skill, and skills expire faster than HR models can update. The learner wins.

3.

What is the new organisation?

Flatter, more fluid, skills-first — where humans lead and AI agents execute: creation through orchestration, not substitution.

4.

Where does HR stand?

Today's HR leader becomes the architect of this transition — deciding who learns what, and how the new org assembles.

01
The Shift

Why work — not just jobs — is being reengineered, and why this time feels different.

Act 1 · ~6 min

The adoption gap · 2026
9 in 10 vs 1 in 5

Nearly 9 in 10 organisations report using AI in at least one function (McKinsey) — yet only about 1 in 5 U.S. workers say any of their work is actually done with AI (Pew, Sept 2025, up from 16%). What companies deploy and what people do at their desk are two very different numbers.

Sources: [1] McKinsey, State of AI in 2026 · [2] Pew Research Center, Sept 2025

Scale of disruption
Up to 30%
of current work hours could be automated by 2030 (McKinsey).

And AI & information processing will affect 86% of businesses by 2030 (WEF). The payoff is real too — the most AI-exposed companies now show 40% higher productivity growth than the least (PwC). It's mostly tasks being automated, not whole jobs vanishing — but that still redefines nearly every role.

Sources: [4] WEF Future of Jobs 2025 · [3] PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer · McKinsey on automation

The deeper truth

Jobs won't vanish in one stroke.
They get rewritten task by task.

What AI is freeing

  • − Routine transactions, drafting, summarising, data sifting
    speed & volume that humans alone can't match
  • − First-draft and pattern work
    the "80% done" heavy lifting

What stays human

  • + Judgement, context, trade-offs
    deciding what's actually right, not just fast
  • + Empathy, relationships, accountability
    the things clients and teams feel

PwC finds the new tasks added to AI-exposed roles are 2.5× more likely to rely on empathy, judgement and creativity — the very skills that grow more valuable as AI absorbs routine work.

Frame: "Human-led, AI-enabled" teams — the productivity gains come from orchestration (see [3] PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer).

02
AI: What's Actually Real

Agents, autonomy, and the shift from tools you ask to helpers that act.

Act 2 · ~9 min

From tool to teammate

AI is moving up the capability ladder

It began as a calculator (you do the work), then a copilot (it drafts, you refine). The next step is an agent — a helper with "hands" that plans and acts on your behalf. Agentic AI is scaling fast: 40% of large-enterprise respondents now report scaling AI agents, up from 27% a year earlier (McKinsey, State of AI 2026).

Assistant

You ask · it answers

Search, summarise, generate — output on demand. The human is fully in the loop.

Copilot

You guide · it drafts

AI proposes, human decides. The dominant pattern of 2024–25 talent workflows.

Agent

You set goal · it acts

Autonomy over defined tasks: schedules, triages, orchestrates tools — with guardrails.

Agent teams

Goals · it manages

Multiple agents per human, coordinated to deliver outcomes — the frontier today.

Modernise vs reimagine

The trap: layering AI onto old work

The safe, common mistake is to bolt AI onto yesterday's processes and call it transformation. Research is blunt: AI creates more jobs than it displaces only if organisations deliberately redesign work — not just add the tool.

Layering (fails)

  • ✗ Same roles, same hand-offs
    AI just accelerates 20th-century structure
  • ✗ The value gap
    only ~6% of orgs qualify as AI "high performers" that see real profit impact (McKinsey 2026)

Redesigning (compounds)

  • ✓ Rebuild the workflow
    humans + agents each do what they do best
  • ✓ Skills-first reskilling
    builts learning into the org, not as a course
03
Skills: The New Currency

When skills expire faster than job descriptions, the educated are those who keep learning.

Act 3 · ~10 min

WEF Future of Jobs 2025
39%
of core skills are expected to change by 2030.

Survey of 1,000+ employers representing 14M+ workers across 22 industries and 55 economies. The headline: skills are now the currency of resilience — the organisations that grow people outperform those that only buy technology.

Source: [4] World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025

What's rising

Top skills employers rank as rising in importance

Analytical & critical thinking#1
AI & big data#2
Creativity & innovation#3
Leadership & social influence#4
Resilience, flexibility & agility#5

Source: [4] WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 — share of employers citing each skill as rising.

Technical + human

The winning mix is T-shaped

→ Depth · technical

  • 1 Working with AI/data
    prompting, AI literacy, data sense
  • 2 Basic algorithm understanding
    not coding — understanding logic
  • 3 Domain expertise
    knowing the business you're in

— Breadth · human

  • 1 Empathy & emotional intelligence
    hardest to automate, rising in demand
  • 2 Communication & collaboration
    consistently the #1 prized skill
  • 3 Judgement & ethics
    owning the decision, not the tool
The new currency · 2026

AI rewards new judgement

42%
faster wage growth for jobs "professionalised" by AI — growing 2× as fast as "democratised" jobs since 2021 (PwC).

WEF projects 59% of the global workforce — over 120M workers — need reskilling or upskilling by 2030, with 11 points' share unlikely to receive it. And the skills for the most AI-exposed jobs change more than 2× as fast as for the least exposed (PwC). Skills truly are the new currency — they expire faster than job titles.

Sources: [3] PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer · [4] WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 press release

04
The New Organisation

Skills-first, fluid teams, and infrastructure for human + AI to work as one.

Act 4 · ~10 min

Structural change

From boxes & lines to teams that assemble

Old org (functional)

  • ◼ Static hierarchy & silos
    decision speed governed by layer count
  • ◼ Roles defined by job titles
    not by what someone can actually do
  • ◼ People assigned, not matched
    talent sits where the chart puts it

New org (skills-first)

  • ◼ Flatter, networked, fluid
    "team of teams," reshaped around work
  • ◼ Skills as the spine
    roles are snapshots of live capability
  • ◼ Talent marketplaces
    people pulled to projects by what they do
Operating model

Human-led, AI-enabled teams

The winning pattern isn't a robot takeover or a human-only world. It's orchestration: humans own goals and judgement; AI agents handle volume, speed and pattern work. Productivity compounds when the two are deliberately designed together, across customer experience, ops and R&D.

Humans

Direction & accountability

Set the goal, judge the output, own the outcome, keep relationships.

AI agents

Execution & scale

Draft, analyse, schedule, triage — orchestrated within guardrails.

Teams

Assembled by objective

Fluid pods formed around outcomes, dissolved when the work is done.

HR

The architect

Builds the skills data, the marketplaces, and the learning loop that makes it work.

What HR runs

The new HR operating system

1

Skills intelligence

Treat learning data like business intelligence — skills inventory, uplift rate, adoption depth.

2

Agile talent marketplaces

Match people to work by live capability, not title — internal mobility as the default.

3

Continuous, embedded learning

Learning inside the workflow, not a once-a-year course. Short-cycle, measurable.

4

Human-first design

AI frees time; HR reinvests it in wellbeing, purpose and meaningful work.

So What Do We Do?

A personal, practical close — plus a myth-busting challenge.

Act 5 · ~10 min

Interactive · quiz

Myth vs fact

Cut through the noise

Myth

"AI will replace entire jobs in one go."

Mostly tasks are automated; roles get redesigned task-by-task. The risk is refusing to change, not the tool itself.

Fact

The adoption gap is the real problem.

~9 in 10 organisations deploy AI, but only ~1 in 5 U.S. workers use it at work ([1] McKinsey · [2] Pew). Closing that gap beats buying more tech.

Myth

"Soft skills don't matter in an AI era."

Backwards — new tasks in AI-heavy roles are 2.5× more likely to need empathy, judgement & creativity ([3] PwC).

Fact

"Buying AI is enough."

AI underdelivers without work redesign and reskilling. Only ~6% of orgs are AI "high performers" that see real profit impact ([1] McKinsey 2026).

Sources: [1] McKinsey, State of AI 2026 · [2] Pew, Sept 2025 · [3] PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer

Your playbook

Five moves for any organisation

01

Audit tasks, not just roles

Map which tasks AI can own and which need humans — before redesigning jobs.

02

Build AI literacy for all

From senior leaders to entry-level: comfort with AI is now a baseline, not a speciality.

03

Make skills visible

Inventories, marketplaces and data — treat capability as an asset you can route.

04

Reskill deliberately

Short-cycle, measurable, embedded in work — not a once-a-year training calendar.

05

Keep humans central

Accountability, empathy and purpose stay human. Design the org around that.
